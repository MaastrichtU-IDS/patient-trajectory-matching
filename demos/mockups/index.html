PATIENT TRAJECTORY MATCHING · INTERFACE MOCKUPS

Trajectory definition and matching for clinical researchers

Every patient here is invented. Terminology codes are illustrative and unverified. These pages explore interaction design; nothing on them is a clinical claim or a product capability.

B · Start from a patient

Open one patient's history, see every record behind each event and what is wrong with it, decide what must match and what only orders the list, and compare two patients side by side over their index windows.

Define a trajectory

Set each patient's index period, then build the trajectory as steps and timing constraints: a sequence, a tree or a graph. Every patient is matched, with exact, possible, relaxed and not-comparable results kept apart.

Store query · MIMIC-IV demo

Build a trajectory from the open MIMIC-IV demo's item classes, with value conditions such as "mean blood pressure below 65 mmHg", and run it in the execution store on the server. Only aggregate counts come back. It needs a store built locally from the demo files; without one the page says how to build it. Unlike the pages above, it uses the open MIMIC-IV demo, not the synthetic cohort.

Roadmap · built, in progress, next

The key capabilities that exist now, what is being built, what comes next, what is deferred until needed, and what needs a decision from the project.

Results · execution store and matching

What the evidence shows so far: a 1,000,000-patient store built and verified under a 4 GB limit, matching at 300,000 patients against a latency target fixed in advance, and a full census of the MIMIC-IV demo's chart events. Every figure is read from a committed report. Unlike the other pages, it reports aggregate counts from the open MIMIC-IV demo alongside synthetic data.

The test dataset

The interfaces are generic; the dataset they run on here is a synthetic paediatric rare-disease cohort: 48 children across Dravet syndrome, GEFS+, febrile seizures, PCDH19, CDKL5, tuberous sclerosis, Rett syndrome and SMA, generated by demos/mockups/synthetic/generate.py. Each patient has a hidden truth timeline and the records sources would hold, with every injected defect listed: missing values and results, unit and decimal errors, transposed dates, duplicates, copied-forward text, misattributed records, approximate and conflicting onsets, late recording and late availability, reclassified variants, preterm corrected age, broader, local and inconsistent codes, and Dutch and French letters with negation, hedging, plans and family history.
